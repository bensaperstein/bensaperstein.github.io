01 / SUMMARY

A professor mentioned a datathon. My friends and I signed up.

I heard about the DSAC Datathon through my professors and took on the challenge with a group of friends. We worked with Philadelphia restaurant data and Yelp reviews, trying to connect the patterns we found with recommendations a restaurant owner could use.

My role: restaurant-level OLS analysis, regression visualizations, substantial presentation work, co-presentation, and joint interpretation. A teammate led most of the sentiment-analysis implementation.

02 / METHODS

Restaurant attributes alongside the words in reviews.

I worked on OLS models relating average stars to restaurant attributes and review-related measures, using an 80/20 train-test split. Regression plots helped turn the model output into a story we could present.

Alongside that work, our team used Word2Vec to expand keywords for food, service, atmosphere, and price; split reviews into sentences with NLTK; and scored sentiment with VADER. The team also explored gradient boosting with LightGBM.

03 / RESULTS

Second place, and plenty the model couldn’t explain.

The restaurant-level model reported a test RMSE of 0.71 stars and R² of about 0.21. Review engagement, recency, and location showed more informative associations than many individual amenities.

Our combined analysis and presentation earned second place among nine teams. The recommendations translated those patterns into ideas for restaurant strategy; they were not tested interventions.

These are the original presentation’s evaluation figures. Further review of feature timing and split design would be needed before treating the model as a validated forecast.

04 / WHAT I LEARNED

Start with a smaller question. Then talk it through.

The problem was intimidating at first. Breaking it into smaller pieces and talking it through with my friends made it easier to find a starting point. I’d been doing that for years with a Rubik’s Cube; here I had to apply the same habit to a much less familiar problem.

Working outside the classroom gave me a better sense of what a data-science project can involve. My regression work and my teammate’s sentiment analysis offered different views of the restaurants, and we had to bring them together into something we could explain. Even then, a recommendation was still an idea to test, rather than an outcome we’d demonstrated.
